AI glossary

An AI glossary, no hype.

The words you'll hear when people talk about AI and automation, each explained in one line. You don't need to know them all. That's my job.

The AI

Generative AI / LLM
The “brain” that reads and writes text, like ChatGPT or Claude.
Prompt
The instructions you give the AI. Written well, they change everything.
Context window
How much the AI can keep in mind at once.
Tokens
The little chunks of text AI reads, and pays by.
Hallucination
When AI makes something up, confidently.
AI agent
AI that doesn't just answer, but takes steps and uses tools.
RAG
Letting AI answer from your documents, not from memory.
Embeddings and vector database
How your information is organised so AI finds what's relevant.
AI model
Each company (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google…) has several, and each shines at something: text, code, images, video or research.
Large and small models
Large ones reason better; small ones are fast and cheap. Efficient means using each where it fits.
Pay per use
Most models charge for every request. With the wrong model at scale, the bill takes off.
Multimodal
AI that understands or creates more than text: images, audio or video.
Human-in-the-loop
The point where a person checks before things move on.
Evals
Tests that check the AI answers well, before and after every change.

Connecting tools

API
The door two programs use to talk to each other.
Webhook
An automatic “something just happened” ping.
JSON
The format data travels in between apps.
MCP
A standard plug that connects AI to your tools.
OAuth
The “sign in with Google” that grants access without sharing passwords.
Trigger
What starts a flow: an email, a form, a time of day.
OCR
Reading text from photos and scanned PDFs.
Low-code / no-code
Tools like n8n, Make or Zapier for building flows without coding everything.
CRM
Where your customers and contacts live.

Security

API keys
The “key” that unlocks a tool. Kept locked away, never in an email.
Secrets manager
The safe where passwords and keys live.
Least privilege
Each piece gets only the access it needs, nothing more.
2FA / MFA
A second step when signing in, like a code on your phone.
SSO
One company login for every tool.
Encryption
Data travels and is stored unreadable to anyone who shouldn't see it.
Prompt injection
Hidden instructions in an email or web page that try to trick the AI.
Data leak
Information ending up where it shouldn't.
Shadow AI
AI tools people use without anyone approving them.
Audit log
The record of who did what, and when.
Anonymisation
Removing names and personal details before AI sees them.
EU data residency
Keeping your data on European servers.
DPA (data processing agreement)
The contract that makes a provider look after your data.
Backup
So one mistake doesn't wipe out your work.

Rules

GDPR
The personal-data rules every automation must respect.
EU AI Act
The EU law on using AI responsibly.
AI literacy
Knowing enough about AI to use it well at work. The AI Act asks companies to support it.

The hard part isn't any one piece. It's knowing how they fit.

An automation that really works brings many of these pieces together. Choosing which ones you need, which you don't and how to connect them so it doesn't break the first time is exactly my job. And if you're curious, I'll explain it along the way.
